## 3. Successes & Strengths

### 3.1 Registry + contract-test pattern (story 1-4) — *Alex's chosen biggest win*

Three-part pattern — Java enum + SQL CHECK + JSON fixture (`PersistedRegistryValues`) synced by `RegistryContractTest`. Propagated organically to 1-9 (`IdempotencyRecordStatus`), 1-10 (`DataClassification`), 1-13 (`RunnerStage`). **Zero registry drift across 23 stories.** Cheapest, highest-leverage architectural guardrail Epic 1 produced.

### 3.2 Idempotency seam (story 1-9) reused without redesign

`IdempotencyRecordPort` + deterministic fingerprinting `(domainKey, stage, version)` reused by 1-12, 1-13, 1-15 with zero redesign. Early coupling traded for downstream consistency. Validates the principle: define abstract-port boundaries once a second consumer exists, and stop there.

### 3.3 Foundation-gate CI (story 1-23) closes Epic 1 invariants

Tiered pipeline + foundation-gate verification means Epic 2-4 feature work cannot regress Epic 1 invariants. Most load-bearing artifact for everything that follows.

### 3.4 Zero critical defects shipped despite heavy review churn

Adversarial review (Blind Hunter / Edge Case Hunter / Acceptance Auditor) caught everything before merge. All 30+ deferred items are **architectural trade-offs** (concurrency design, query performance, cross-platform hardening), not bugs.

### 3.5 Disciplined sprint-change in flight

Story 1-12 scope drift was caught mid-sprint. Result: sprint-change proposal (2026-05-11) narrowed 1-12 to fail-closed skeleton, deferred lineage repair to Epic 4, descoped 1-20, and inserted 1-12c to harden N+1 / UPDATE-REPLACE risks before story 1-13 exercised them. Contained, traceable, converted into work.

No columns dropped or renamed across the epic. Schema evolved cleanly. Story 1-12c added V4 unique-constraint widening without data cleanup. Pattern to keep.

## 4. Challenges & Growth Areas

### 4.1 Story 1-12 scope drift + chunked reviews — *Alex's chosen biggest pain*

Largest story by far. 276 KB current.diff, 5+ review rounds, sprint-change required, follow-up 1-12c inserted. **Root cause:** boundary between "skeleton" (fail-closed safety) and "operator-facing recovery" (lineage repair, ambiguous-replay semantics) was blurry in the story spec itself. The team kept pulling adjacent concerns into the story.

**Fix:** Skeleton Completeness Checklist (Action A2) — three questions at story top: does this stop at fail-closed safety; what is the single most adjacent concern marked out-of-scope; what is the trigger that signals scope drift mid-development. Plus Chunk Plan template (A3) for stories tagged 'large' at creation.

### 4.2 Story 1-21 chunked CI review (1.5 MB of patches)

Tiered pipeline + branch protection + bundled-jar smoke was too large to review atomically. ChunkA / ChunkB / ChunkC semantic split happened *mid-review* rather than being planned upfront. Outcome was good; cost was avoidable.

### 4.3 Cross-platform constraints discovered too late (story 1-17)

Windows + Linux Docker strategy, scripting, and probe-logic differences surfaced at story 1-17. Stories 1-1 through 1-16 had already hardened Linux-only assumptions. Windows doctor-smoke was collapsed to Ubuntu-only as a pragmatic unblock.

### 4.4 Snapshot-test maintenance burden

Byte-exact snapshots in 1-15, 1-18, 1-22, 1-23 stabilized the foundation but accumulate maintenance cost — any output-format tweak breaks 30+ snapshots. Need explicit guidance to avoid the pattern in Epic 2's frontend tests.

### 4.5 Concurrency-design debt (F16/F17/F18) acknowledged but unresolved

Eager-COMPLETED idempotency write + dropped `@Lock(PESSIMISTIC_READ)` annotation + retry-state read outside transaction. Fine for CLI pilot (constraint-based contention serves as backstop), but Epic 2 introduces REST mutations and concurrent frontend requests. Will need a concurrency-hardening story before production load.

### 4.6 Redaction policy gaps deferred

F19/F20: `Idempotency-Key` header not redacted, PEM blocks not pattern-matched, bundle JSON shapes uncovered. Acceptable for CLI pilot but **upstream of Epic 2 story 2-24** (artifact content sanitization for untrusted runner output). See Significant Discovery below.

## 5. Key Insights & Learnings

### Pattern: Architectural guardrails (registry recipe, idempotency seam) scale; output-shape snapshot tests don't

The patterns that produced zero drift / zero defects were *invariant guards* — they assert structural properties (enum value sets match, idempotency keys serialize deterministically). The patterns that produced maintenance burden were *output-shape snapshots* — they assert exact bytes of strings that change for reasonable reasons. Invest in the former; constrain the latter to true presentation surfaces.

### Pattern: Adversarial review caught under-specification, not bugs

All review findings were about contract clarity, ordering invariants, or method-naming drift — not coding errors. The Blind Hunter / Edge Case Hunter / Acceptance Auditor framework is doing real work, but the cost is real (2-3 review rounds per large story). Upstream specification clarity (Skeleton Completeness Checklist) will compound.

### Pattern: Mid-flight tech-debt insertions work when narrowly scoped

Story 1-12c (3 ACs, 4 tasks) inserted between 1-12 and 1-13 was effective because it removed blockers for downstream stories. Insertion is a valid response to scope correction when the inserted story is *itself* tightly bounded.

### Pattern: Define abstract ports when a second consumer appears

Idempotency port (1-9) was defined because 1-12 needed it; then 1-13 and 1-15 reused it. The right time to abstract is at the second consumer, not the first.

### Pattern: Cross-platform parity must be wired from story 1, not retrofitted

Windows-on-Linux gaps in stories 1-1 through 1-16 cost story 1-17 disproportionate effort and forced a pragmatic Ubuntu-only retreat. Wire matrix parity in story 2-1.

## 11. Key Takeaways

1. **Registry + contract-test pattern is the cheapest, highest-leverage guardrail we shipped.** Canonize it as the Registry Recipe and apply it to every new state vocabulary in Epic 2.
2. **Skeleton vs. operator-facing-recovery boundary clarity** is the single most important upstream specification discipline going forward.
3. **Adversarial review caught under-specification, not bugs.** Invest in spec clarity to reduce review rounds; the framework is doing real work but at high cost per large story.
4. **Cross-platform parity must be wired from story 1.** We learned this twice in Epic 1 (story 1-17). We will not learn it a third time.
5. **The redaction-policy gap reaches into Epic 2's threat model.** Story 2-24 must close F19/F20, not just sanitize HTML.
